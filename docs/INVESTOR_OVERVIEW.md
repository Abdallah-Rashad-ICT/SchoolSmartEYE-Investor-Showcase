# SchoolSmartEYE AI — Investor Overview

## The opportunity

Schools need useful digital intelligence, but AI adoption can be constrained by connectivity, infrastructure cost, data governance, and fragmented systems. SchoolSmartEYE AI is being developed around a local-first architecture intended to bring selected AI capabilities closer to the school.

## The product vision

One coherent school intelligence ecosystem combining:
- a distributed local AI router;
- educational AI and retrieval over approved school knowledge;
- computer-vision and temporal safety research;
- cautious predictive risk indicators for human review;
- a potential opt-in wearable pathway, subject to strict governance.

The initial target is one school on its own local network. Multi-school expansion is a later stage, not the starting assumption.

## The infrastructure idea

The Distributed Local AI Router is intended to coordinate eligible workloads across ordinary computers already available in a school.

- **PC1 — School Core:** application and policy coordination.
- **PC2–PC4 — Optional local workers:** future enrolled compute or specialized processing nodes.
- **PC5 — Candidate local AI services:** local models, embeddings, and knowledge retrieval.

This is an illustrative role map. It is not evidence of a validated five-device production deployment. Authenticated end-to-end execution, node enrollment, hardware benchmarks, and LAN acceptance remain engineering milestones.

## Why local-first?

The architecture aims to reduce reliance on continuous internet connectivity, support school-controlled data boundaries, and make practical use of existing hardware where benchmarks demonstrate suitability. Actual savings, latency, reliability, and hardware requirements must be measured in a representative pilot.

## Current maturity

The current engineering repository contains foundational security and local-routing primitives plus experimental capability work. It is not yet a complete production school application.

Computer vision and temporal/fall-related capabilities remain experimental. Predictive risk intelligence is a research direction without validated accuracy. Wearable integration is blocked pending redesign and governance. The local router is not yet an authenticated end-to-end PC1-to-PC5 execution system.

## Proposed validation milestones

1. Stabilize the local core, identity, authorization, and school boundaries.
2. Complete authenticated service-to-service execution and durable replay protection.
3. Build reproducible local model and hardware benchmarks on ordinary school computers.
4. Integrate a minimal school workflow and approved-document RAG path.
5. Run a controlled pilot using synthetic or explicitly approved data.
6. Evaluate usability, privacy, reliability, safety, cost, and latency before expanding scope.

## Investment framing

The opportunity is a differentiated local-first architecture and a staged programme to validate it in a real school environment. Funding milestones should be tied to measurable acceptance criteria—not unsupported claims of production readiness or performance.
